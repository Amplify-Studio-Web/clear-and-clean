Sprinkler spots stay on your windows because the water leaves minerals behind when it dries. Those minerals bond to the glass, so a quick rinse or a household spray does not lift them. The longer the spots sit and the more often the sprinklers hit the glass, the harder they are to remove.

If your lower windows look cloudy or speckled while the upper ones look fine, irrigation is a likely cause. Here is what is going on and what helps.

# What are those white spots on my windows?

Tap and well water carries dissolved minerals. When sprinkler spray lands on a window and evaporates, the water leaves those minerals on the surface as small white or gray spots. Across DFW, where lawns are watered often through the warm months, ground-floor glass near sprinkler heads can get hit several times a week.

At first the spots are light and wipe away fairly easily. Over time, layer builds on layer. The deposits harden and start to etch into the glass, which is why old spots can be very stubborn.

# Why doesn't regular window cleaner remove them?

Most household glass cleaners are made for dust, fingerprints and light film. Mineral deposits are a different problem. They are bonded to the glass, so wiping just moves the cleaner over the top of them.

Scrubbing hard with the wrong tool can make things worse. Abrasive pads and razor blades used carelessly can scratch glass or damage coatings on some windows. If the spots are heavy or have been there a long time, it is safer to have them handled by someone who works on glass every day.

# How can you stop the spots from coming back?

Cleaning removes the spots you have now. Preventing new ones comes down to keeping water off the glass:

- **Adjust the sprinkler heads.** Many heads can be turned or have their spray pattern changed so they water the lawn instead of the house.
- **Check after mowing and repairs.** Heads get bumped by mowers and foot traffic and drift toward the windows over time.
- **Water at the right time.** Early morning watering is common advice for lawns, and it also gives glass a chance to dry without sitting in afternoon heat.
- **Watch for broken heads.** A cracked or stuck head can spray straight up and hit second-story glass too.
- **Keep up with regular cleaning.** Spots that are removed while they are fresh do not get the chance to harden and build up.

# Do sprinkler spots damage windows permanently?

They can if they are left long enough. Mineral deposits that sit for a long time can etch the glass surface, leaving marks that cleaning cannot fully remove. That is the main reason to deal with spots sooner rather than later and to fix the sprinkler spray that causes them.

# Frequently asked questions

## Why are only my lower windows spotted?

Sprinklers usually reach only the bottom of the house, so ground-floor glass near the lawn and flower beds takes most of the spray.

## Can I use vinegar on sprinkler spots?

Mild acidic cleaners can help with light, fresh spots. Heavy or old deposits usually need professional treatment, and harsh scrubbing can scratch glass.

## Will the spots come back after a cleaning?

They will if the sprinklers keep hitting the glass. Adjusting the heads and keeping a regular cleaning schedule is the best way to keep windows clear.
